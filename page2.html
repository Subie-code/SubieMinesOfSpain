As one of Iowa's designated Watchable Wildlife Areas, the Mines of Spain supports a surprising range of animal life across its bluffs, wetlands, and prairie. Visitors have a good chance of spotting songbirds, bobcats, red-shouldered hawks, flying squirrels, white-tailed deer, wild turkey, and a variety of butterflies along the Mississippi River bluffland. Overhead, bald eagles are a common sight along the Mississippi River corridor, and the park's mix of forest, wetland, and tallgrass prairie habitats makes it a magnet for migratory birds in spring and fall. The 250-year-old oak trees on the prairie and the Betty Hauptli Bird and Butterfly Garden add extra draw for birders and pollinator-watchers alike, and the park even includes a dedicated wildlife observation blind for quieter, closer viewing.
