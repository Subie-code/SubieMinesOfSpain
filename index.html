Mines of Spain

Mines of Spain State Recreation Area sits on Dubuque's southern edge along the Mississippi River, covering roughly 1,439 acres and managed by the Iowa Department of Natural Resources since its 1981 establishment. It's named for a 1796 land grant: Julien Dubuque, credited as the first European to settle in Iowa in 1788, received permission from the Spanish governor to work a roughly 189-square-mile area that became known as the "Mines of Spain," building on lead deposits Native Americans had long known and that French explorer Nicholas Perrot first revealed to Europeans around 1690; mining and smelting continued there into the early 1900s. The park today blends that history with nature, offering picnic areas, 16.5 miles of walking and hiking trails, 3.5 miles of ski trails, and the Betty Hauptli Bird and Butterfly Garden, along with the Julien Dubuque Monument (built in 1897), the Horseshoe Bluff Interpretive Area with its rock cliffs, wetland, and prairies, and the E.B. Lyons Interpretive Center serving as visitor center and park office — the archaeological sites tied to the early lead mining venture are collectively designated a National Historic Landmark District as "Julien Dubuque's Mines." Beyond hiking, visitors can cross-country ski in winter on trails groomed for both classical and skate skiing, and the area permits trapping and archery hunting in all seasons, with limited firearm hunting for deer and spring turkey south of Catfish Creek; leashed dogs (six feet or shorter) are welcome, and the park stays open year-round from 4 a.m. to 10:30 p.m.
